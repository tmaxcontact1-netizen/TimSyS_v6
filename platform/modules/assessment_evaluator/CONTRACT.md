# Assessment Evaluator component contract

Assessment Evaluator independently determines the intellectual work an assessment item requires before it is allowed to inspect or map standards. Claimed alignment, answer keys, official depth labels, scoring guidance and benchmark classifications remain sealed until the item's analysis is locked.

The component distinguishes the primary construct from supporting skills, records observable evidence behaviour, accounts for scaffolding, and separates alignment from depth. Automated rules may recommend and explain; they may not force an alignment or award high confidence. Ambiguity and insufficient evidence are valid results. A human reviewer retains final authority, and every revision remains auditable.

Teacher mode accepts a question paper as the sole required input. Coordinator mode may add an answer key, rubric, specification, declared objectives and one or more comparison frameworks. Graphs, charts, pictures and notation are represented as student-visible media attached to the item; an item is not considered dependably analysed until those relationships have been extracted or professionally described.

The shared AI gateway may be invoked only after deterministic analysis is locked and only when that analysis is ambiguous, insufficient or explicitly requires human review. Its bounded evidence, citations, provider, model, confidence and limitations are retained. AI output is a proposal and cannot overwrite the classification or standards mapping; a professional must accept, revise or reject it.

Declared intent remains sealed until every item has a locked construct analysis and standards mapping. Mapping consumes verified, versioned ontology nodes and can compare multiple frameworks, including explicitly selected cross-subject frameworks. Generated reports separate quantitative distributions, qualitative interpretation, review queues, recommendations, limitations and method provenance.

Reports include item-level findings, construct and confidence distributions, framework coverage, standards frequencies, visual-evidence and review counts, optional intent comparison, prioritised actions and machine-readable exports. Reports describe evidence and uncertainty; they do not certify assessment quality automatically.

This component evaluates assessment instruments. It does not calculate student grades and does not evaluate curriculum scope or sequence. Its locked construct, alignment and report records form a stable contract that a future Curriculum Evaluator may consume.
