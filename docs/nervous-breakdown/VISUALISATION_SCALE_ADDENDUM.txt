Nervous Breakdown — Layer 1 Visualisation and Scale Architecture Addendum
This instruction supersedes the previous proposed Layer 1 visualisation addendum.
It adds two significant requirements to the Nervous Breakdown design specification:
1. Layer 1 must ultimately support coordinated 3D Network, 2D Network, and List representations.
2. The current seven JDs are an initial corpus only. The architecture must anticipate substantial expansion across the organisation.
This does not change the current v0.2.1 validation/import sequence or authorise Layer 2/3 implementation.
1. Expected organisational scale
Do not architect Nervous Breakdown around the current corpus size.
The initial corpus contains:
- 7 JDs;
- 328 source statements;
- 1,195 atomic responsibilities;
- 179 ontology terms;
- 167 current endpoint-complete analytical connections;
- 95 unresolved documentary role handoffs.
BBS is a much larger organisation.
The immediate expectation is that the source corpus may expand to approximately:
20–30 job descriptions

and potentially further.
Future source types may additionally include:
- policies;
- procedures;
- SOPs;
- handbooks;
- regulatory requirements;
- approved operational documents.
Therefore, for architectural planning, assume Nervous Breakdown should comfortably support at least:
5,000 atomic responsibility nodes

and:
tens of thousands of relationships

without requiring fundamental redesign.
These are design-capacity targets, not expected initial display counts.

4. Existing unresolved handoffs may become resolvable
The current corpus contains 95 unresolved documentary role handoffs.
These are not necessarily permanent defects.
Some may exist because:
JD A explicitly points to Role B,

but:
Role B's own JD is not currently available or does not yet provide a defensible receiving responsibility.

As additional JDs are ingested, Nervous Breakdown should be capable of identifying that a previously unresolved organisational endpoint may now have candidate responsibility nodes.
However:
do not resolve the handoff automatically.

The system may eventually flag:
New responsibilities now exist that may resolve unresolved handoff H-017.

A human reviews and approves the connection.
The historical unresolved state should remain auditable.
5. Ontology growth
The current ontology is provisional and derived from seven JDs.
Do not assume 179 terms represent the final organisational ontology.
When new JDs are analysed:
1. attempt classification against the existing ontology;
2. reuse existing terms where they genuinely fit;
3. do not create synonyms merely because wording differs;
4. do not force genuinely novel responsibilities into inappropriate existing categories;
5. allow governed ontology expansion where necessary.
The ontology should become progressively more mature as the source corpus expands.
6. Ontology stability
Although the ontology must remain editable, growth should increasingly favour stability.
Later JDs should normally reuse established concepts rather than continuously fragment the taxonomy.
Future governance/Insights may identify:
- synonymous ontology terms;
- excessively broad categories;
- overly narrow categories;
- underused terms;
- conflicting classifications.
Do not implement automated ontology consolidation now.
7. One Layer 1 graph
There is only one Layer 1 organisational graph.
The graph consists fundamentally of:

governed relationships between them.

Do not create separate graph models for:
- Middle School;
- High School;
- Elementary;
- departments;
- domains;
- roles;
- source types.
Those may become filters, clusters, views, or organisational partitions over the same underlying graph.

10. 3D Network — primary signature view
The intended primary visualisation is a fully interactive three-dimensional organisational responsibility network.
The interaction metaphor is:
an interactive model of a solar system or constellation.

The user should feel they are navigating the organisation spatially.
The 3D view should ultimately support:
- free 360-degree rotation;
- smooth zoom;
- pan;
- recenter;
- node selection;
- search;
- responsibility selection;
- camera focus;
- trail highlighting;
- contextual fading;
- node inspection;
- reset to global organisation;
- navigation between connected responsibilities.
Desktop/laptop only.
Mobile optimisation is not required.

12. Organisational geography
At larger scale, the 3D graph should allow recognisable organisational regions to emerge.
For example:

may form one region;

another;

another;

Cross-functional responsibilities may form bridges between them.
The user should eventually develop a spatial understanding of the organisation.
13. Spatial stability is important
Do not randomly regenerate the organisation's spatial arrangement during ordinary use.
Users may develop spatial memory:
"Safeguarding is through there."

"Student support wraps around behaviour."

"Assessment sits near teaching."

That is valuable.
Prefer deterministic/stable positioning or persisted derived positions after graph layout stabilisation.
Positions remain presentation data, not organisational truth.
14. Global constellation
The complete graph should remain available.
The user should be able to request:
Show All

and see the organisation as a whole.
With 3,000–5,000 nodes this may be visually intimidating.
That is acceptable.
Do not remove valid responsibility nodes merely to make the organisation appear simpler.
15. Progressive disclosure
Large-scale usability should be achieved through progressive visual disclosure, not destruction of underlying complexity.
At maximum zoom-out:
- major clusters are apparent;
- individual labels are limited;
- edges may be subtle;
- organisational shape dominates.
At intermediate zoom:
- roles/subdomains become clearer;
- relevant local relationships become more visible.
At close zoom:
- individual responsibility nodes become readable;
- local edges become clear;
- detailed interaction becomes possible.
This is conceptually similar to geographic mapping:
the underlying roads exist even when the national view does not label every street.

16. Edge-density management
At future scale, edge count may be a greater challenge than node count.
Do not assume every relationship should be rendered with equal prominence simultaneously.
Possible visual behaviour:
Global
Nodes visible; most edges subdued.
Domain/role focus
Relevant edges become clearer.
Responsibility selected
Relevant trail becomes dominant.
Close local view
Additional neighbouring connections appear.
The underlying graph remains unchanged.
17. Do not confuse visibility with existence
If an edge is visually suppressed because of:
- zoom;
- filter;
- performance optimisation;
- progressive disclosure;
that does not mean the connection has been removed from the graph.
Visual state and organisational state must remain separate.

22. Node semantics
The fundamental graph node remains:
atomic responsibility

Do not solve scale by replacing responsibility nodes with roles.
Roles may provide:
- clustering;
- labels;
- filtering;
- grouping;
- metadata;
- spatial influence.
But the Layer 1 graph remains responsibility-based.
23. Optional visual aggregation
At very large scale, the renderer may visually aggregate distant nodes for performance/legibility.
For example:
127 responsibilities

might appear as a distant cluster representation until zoomed.
However:
- the underlying responsibilities remain individually represented in data;
- zoom/focus must reveal them;
- search must locate an individual responsibility;
- aggregation must not alter responsibility identity;
- aggregation must not become a separate organisational model.
This is visual level-of-detail only.
24. Organisational sections

- Middle School;

- Student Affairs;
- support services;
- operations.
Do not hard-code the current Middle School perspective into the fundamental responsibility model.

27. Accessibility
No information should be available only through 3D navigation.
Anything represented spatially must remain accessible through 2D/List/inspectors where appropriate.
This is both an accessibility and analytical requirement.
28. Connection provenance
Visualisation must preserve the distinction between:
- documentary;
- inferred;
- proposed;
- approved operational;
- unresolved;
- superseded.
An inferred relationship must not visually masquerade as documentary organisational truth.
The same semantic distinction should apply in both 2D and 3D.

The user should be able to recognise:

As additional JDs are ingested, those grey/unmapped areas may progressively become populated.
This should make organisational completeness itself visually observable.

- inferred edges;

Preserve the data necessary to support it.

Although current Layer 1 is JD-focused, future sources may add further organisational obligations.

from:

34. Performance target
The Layer 1 rendering architecture should be selected with at least the following planning target:
5,000 responsibility nodes

and:
tens of thousands of potential relationships

on a normal contemporary desktop/laptop environment.
This does not mean all edges must be simultaneously rendered at full visual complexity.
Use appropriate:
- GPU acceleration;
- WebGL;
- level-of-detail;
- progressive edge visibility;
- spatial indexing;
- graph filtering;
- efficient state updates.
Avoid DOM-heavy rendering approaches that inherently assume a few hundred nodes.
35. Performance must be tested against realistic graph shapes
Synthetic tests should eventually include:
- several thousand nodes;
- sparse regions;
- dense regions;
- highly connected hubs;
- orphan nodes;
- large trails;
- cross-domain edges;
- many faded background nodes;
- repeated filtering;
- rapid view switching.
Do not benchmark only evenly distributed random graphs.
Organisational networks are unlikely to behave that way.
36. Future graph metrics
Before final 3D layout implementation, analyse the real expanded graph for:
- node count;

- degree distribution;
- high-degree hubs;
- isolated nodes;
- connected components;
- domain clustering;
- role clustering;
- authority distribution;
- typical trail size;
- maximum trail size;
- unresolved endpoint density.
Use these findings to choose the final spatial algorithm.
37. Do not implement final spatial semantics prematurely
The current seven JDs are too small a sample of the whole organisation to determine the definitive 3D geography.
Build the capability to support meaningful spatial placement.
Do not permanently decide:
X axis = domain
Y axis = authority
Z axis = role

before seeing the expanded graph.
The final layout should emerge from actual organisational data and usability testing.

49. Default visualisation must remain intelligible
Although Nervous Breakdown deliberately exposes complexity, the default view should not require configuration before it becomes meaningful.
When the user first opens Layer 1, the application should provide a sensible organisational representation immediately.

Do not make the user construct the graph manually.
50. 3D orientation aids
Because free 3D rotation can cause spatial disorientation, the final implementation should consider orientation aids.
Potential examples:
- subtle vertical axis;
- level markers;
- domain legend;
- minimap/overview;
- home orientation;
- reset camera;
- selected-node locator;
- breadcrumb/context indicator.
Do not clutter the graph unnecessarily.
The user should always have an obvious way to return to a known organisational orientation.
51. Selection persistence
A selected responsibility should remain selected until:
- the user selects another responsibility;
- explicitly clears selection;
- changes to an incompatible dataset/version.
Merely rotating, zooming, switching 2D/3D/List, or opening the inspector should not clear the analytical context.
52. Multi-selection — future capability
Do not implement now unless naturally supported.
Future analysis may benefit from selecting multiple responsibilities and asking:
How are these connected?

For example:
Teacher behaviour responsibility

and:
Principal behaviour responsibility.

The system could identify paths between them.
The architecture should not make multi-selection impossible.
53. Pathfinding — future capability
The graph may eventually support deterministic questions such as:
Show the shortest documented path between Responsibility A and Responsibility B.

or:
Show all documentary paths from Teacher to Principal within Behaviour.

or:
Show paths that include inferred relationships.

This could become particularly useful once thousands of nodes exist.
Do not implement pathfinding as part of the current visualisation addendum unless the current graph library provides it trivially.
54. Unresolved-path analysis
Future pathfinding should also be capable of identifying:
path terminates at unresolved handoff.

That is analytically meaningful.
A broken trail should not be silently bridged.
55. Graph growth after new source ingestion
When new JDs are added, the user should not need to rebuild the visualisation.
Once new governed responsibilities and connections become active:
the Layer 1 graph expands automatically.

Likewise:
- 3D updates;
- 2D updates;
- List updates;
- filters update;
- search updates;
- cluster statistics update.
The visualisations are projections of the organisational data.
56. Incremental layout
At larger scale, adding a new JD should ideally not completely scramble the existing spatial map.
If practical, layout should support incremental placement:
existing organisational geography remains broadly stable;

new responsibilities find appropriate locations;

local areas adjust as required.

This preserves spatial memory.
Do not prioritise perfect global graph optimisation over usability continuity.
57. Major remapping
There will nevertheless be occasions when major organisational changes justify recalculating the graph layout.
Examples:
- large-scale JD revision;
- organisational restructuring;
- substantial ontology redesign;
- introduction of many new source types.
The system should be capable of recalculating derived positions when deliberately requested.
Previous organisational versions should remain historically recoverable through the underlying version architecture.
58. Version-aware visualisation
Future Layer 1 should eventually be capable of selecting an organisational version/time state.
For example:

2026–27

versus:
2027–28.

The graph should then derive from records effective in the selected organisational state.
This is future functionality.
Do not implement temporal graph navigation now.
59. Future comparison mode
A later capability may compare two organisational versions visually.
Potentially show:
- added responsibilities;
- removed responsibilities;
- modified responsibilities;
- new connections;
- removed connections;
- role changes;
- density changes;
- resolved gaps.
This could make Nervous Breakdown useful for organisational-change analysis.
Preserve versioning foundations.
60. Performance degradation strategy
If the graph eventually exceeds the comfortable interactive limits of the chosen renderer, degradation should occur gracefully.
Prefer:
1. reduce background edge rendering;
2. use level-of-detail;
3. aggregate distant visual clusters;
4. delay nonessential labels;
5. restrict expensive effects;
6. load/render local neighbourhood detail progressively;
before:
removing responsibilities from the organisational model.

Data completeness is more important than simultaneously rendering every visual detail.
61. No artificial hard cap tied to initial corpus
Do not create assumptions such as:
maximum 1,500 responsibilities

because the current corpus contains approximately 1,200.
Where a practical technical limit is unavoidable, document it and ensure it is comfortably above the planned capacity target.
62. Data retrieval at larger scale
As the corpus grows, avoid architectural assumptions requiring the entire detailed record payload for every responsibility to be loaded into the browser simply to draw a node.
Where appropriate, distinguish:
graph topology/summary data

from:
full responsibility/source details loaded for inspection.

This should follow existing Principal'Ed architecture and actual performance evidence.
Do not prematurely optimise if current architecture already handles the target scale comfortably.
63. Graph identity
Stable responsibility IDs are critical.
Spatial position, selection, trail state, operational overlays, and future graph analytics should reference stable responsibility identity rather than display wording.
A wording revision should not unnecessarily create a visually unrelated object if governance determines it is a version of the same underlying responsibility.
Follow the established versioning model.
64. Graph integrity after ontology changes
Changing a responsibility's domain/subdomain classification may affect:
- cluster placement;
- colour;
- filtering;
- graph geography.
It must not alter the responsibility's identity or documentary provenance.
Visual changes are derived consequences of governed classification changes.
65. Graph integrity after connection changes
Approving, superseding, or resolving a connection should update all three views automatically.

66. Relationship density as information
High connectivity may itself be organisationally meaningful.
Do not automatically interpret it as bad data.
A highly connected responsibility may represent:
- genuine coordination hub;
- leadership bottleneck;
- specialist bridge;
- broad accountability;
- overly vague JD wording;
- duplicated organisational responsibility.
Future Insights may analyse this.
The visualisation should expose connectivity without making evaluative conclusions automatically.
67. Isolated nodes as information
Likewise, isolated responsibilities may indicate:
- legitimate standalone responsibility;
- missing connection;
- missing source document;
- poorly specified JD;
- incomplete mapping.
Do not automatically connect isolated nodes to make the graph visually coherent.

- role;
- domain;

70. AI remains separate
Expansion to thousands of nodes does not change the deterministic-first principle.

- explaining the graph;
- suggesting connections;
- interpreting clusters;

and:
resolution/completeness.

139. New JDs should trigger governance review opportunities
When a new JD is added, the system may eventually identify:
existing unresolved handoffs now pointing toward the newly mapped role;

existing inferred connections potentially supported by new documentary evidence;

ontology terms now used more broadly;

apparent conflicts between old and new source material.

Do not automatically resolve these.
Surface them for governance review.
140. Existing inferred edges may mature
An inferred connection may later acquire documentary support.
Conceptually:
INFERRED
   │
new JD/source added
   │
documentary evidence found
   │
governance review
   │
   ▼
DOCUMENTARY / EXPLICIT

Do not simply overwrite history.
The governance model should preserve how the evidentiary status changed.
141. Existing connections may also be disproved
Likewise, new source material may reveal that an inferred relationship was incorrect.
That connection should be capable of becoming:
superseded/rejected

without deleting the historical analytical record where appropriate.
This is another reason AI or similarity analysis must never automatically define organisational truth.
142. No requirement for perfect completeness before launch
Nervous Breakdown does not need every BBS JD before it becomes useful.
The initial seven-JD graph can launch as a clearly scoped:
current mapped corpus

provided the UI makes mapping completeness clear.
Additional JDs can progressively expand the organisation.
143. Launch corpus versus architecture
Distinguish:
Launch corpus
Whatever governed source material has actually been imported and reviewed when the module launches.
from:
Architectural capacity
The system must be capable of much larger institutional coverage.
Do not delay architecture for scale.
Do not delay usefulness waiting for complete institutional documentation.
144. Current seven-JD graph is a proving ground
The current corpus should be used to test:
- graph integrity;
- trail behaviour;
- ontology;
- evidence semantics;
- unresolved handoffs;
- filters;
- shared state;
- 2D usability;
- List usability.
It should not be used to permanently determine:
- institutional spatial geography;
- final cluster layout;
- maximum graph size;
- final ontology boundaries.

146. Do not rerun the whole organisation unnecessarily
Adding JD #21 should not require re-extracting and reimporting JDs #1–20 merely to add the new role.
Cross-analysis may identify new relationships involving existing responsibilities, but the existing documentary responsibility records remain valid unless their own source/version changes.
147. Reanalysis is different from re-ingestion
The system should conceptually distinguish:
new source ingestion

from:
analytical re-evaluation of the graph.

A new JD may cause us to reconsider relationships among existing responsibilities without changing those responsibilities themselves.
This distinction will become increasingly important as the corpus grows.
148. Future batch analysis
At larger scale, external deterministic/AI-assisted analytical tooling may propose:
- candidate relationships;
- ontology reuse;
- overlaps;
- unresolved-handoff resolutions.
Such tooling should produce reviewable candidate data.
It must not bypass governance.
149. Data portability remains important
As the corpus becomes institution-wide, Nervous Breakdown's structured organisational data becomes increasingly valuable.
Maintain practical export capability for:
- sources;
- statements;
- responsibilities;
- ontology;
- connections;
- versions;
- governance status.
The visualisation itself is replaceable.
The structured organisational model is the durable asset.
150. Final launch architecture statement
The target is not:
a 3D diagram of seven job descriptions.

The target is:
a scalable, governed, institution-wide organisational responsibility graph capable of being explored spatially, relationally, and analytically.

The current seven JDs are the first mapped region.
Twenty or thirty JDs should enlarge the same organisational universe.
Future policies and procedures should enrich the same organisational universe.
Layer 2 should extract understandable processes from that universe.
Layer 3 should eventually allow real work to move through those processes.
